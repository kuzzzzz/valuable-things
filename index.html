10 Valuable Things

Why they matter — and how their value reaches someone who just needs a credit alert or a little money to get through the week.

For the person next to you right now: These are not just rich-people topics. Gold, oil, chips, land and knowledge quietly decide the price of food, fuel, data, rent and the interest rate on the loan you may need. Understanding them is one way to stop feeling like the system is pure magic (or pure unfairness) and start seeing the levers that actually move money.

01

Gold

Why it is valuable

Scarce, durable, and trusted for thousands of years. Governments cannot print more of it. Central banks still hold it. When people lose faith in paper money, gold usually holds purchasing power better than cash.

Why it matters to someone just trying to get by

When inflation rises, the naira (or any currency) buys less food and transport. Gold is one of the signals central banks and big investors watch. Those decisions eventually show up in interest rates, the cost of loans, and how fast prices climb. You may never own a gold bar, but the forces that move gold help decide how hard it is to stretch the money you already have.

How the value developed

c. 4500 BCE
Earliest worked gold (Varna). Already a prestige material.
c. 550 BCE
First pure gold coins in Lydia — gold becomes formal money.
1816–1873
Classical gold standard spreads; currencies linked to gold.
1971 – now
Dollar no longer convertible to gold. Gold becomes a free-floating hedge against inflation and crisis.
02

Diamonds

Why it is valuable

Hard and beautiful, but much of the modern price was created by deliberate scarcity (De Beers) and one of the most successful marketing campaigns in history: “A Diamond Is Forever.”

Why it matters to someone just trying to get by

Diamonds show how marketing can turn a stone into a social requirement. Engagement-ring pressure still pushes people to spend months of income on something that often loses value the moment it leaves the shop. Understanding this helps you decide whether a purchase is really about love — or about an industry that spent decades teaching us what love should look like.

How the value developed

1860s–1888
South African mines flood the market. De Beers is formed to control supply.
1938–1948
“A Diamond Is Forever” campaign invents the modern engagement-ring tradition.
2000s–now
Lab-grown diamonds challenge the old monopoly, yet cultural pressure remains strong.
03

Crude Oil

Why it is valuable

The densest widely available energy source. It powers transport, industry, plastics, fertilisers and chemicals. Control of oil has shaped politics and wealth for over a century.

Why it matters to someone just trying to get by

Oil price moves hit the cost of fuel, generators, transport of food, and many imported goods almost immediately. When oil jumps, the price of rice, bus fares and pure water often follows within days or weeks. Even if you never buy a barrel of oil, you feel it every time you fill a tank, pay for delivery, or buy packaged food.

How the value developed

1859
First commercial oil well (Titusville). Modern industry begins.
1885–1920s
Cars turn gasoline from waste product into the main driver of demand.
1960 & 1973
OPEC forms; oil embargoes show how supply shocks raise prices everywhere.
Today
Still central to transport and petrochemicals even as renewables grow.
04

Bitcoin

Why it is valuable

Fixed supply of 21 million. No central bank can print more. It is the first widely adopted form of digital scarcity — often called “digital gold.”

Why it matters to someone just trying to get by

Bitcoin is extremely volatile — people have made and lost money quickly. For someone who needs cash for rent or food this week, it is usually a high-risk gamble, not a solution. Its deeper relevance is that it forces a question: what happens when people lose trust in banks or local currency? Some turn to alternatives. Understanding the risk helps you avoid putting money you cannot afford to lose into something that can drop 50% in months.

How the value developed

2008–2009
White paper and genesis block. Price starts near zero.
2013–2021
Major bull runs and crashes. Mainstream attention grows.
2024–2025
U.S. spot ETFs approved; price reaches new highs. Still highly volatile.
05

Spices

Why they were valuable

In the pre-modern world, spices preserved food, flavoured bland diets, and signalled extreme wealth. Transport costs and middlemen made a pound of pepper cost several days’ skilled wages.

Why it matters to someone just trying to get by

Spices are a clear historical lesson: when a few people control a scarce, desirable good, they can extract huge profits while ordinary people pay high prices or go without. The same pattern still appears today in fuel, foreign exchange, data, and medicine. Understanding how scarcity and control create price is useful whether you are buying rice or trying to start a small trade.

How the value developed

Ancient–Medieval
Arab, Indian and Chinese traders move spices across long routes.
15th–17th centuries
Europeans fight for direct sea routes. Dutch and Portuguese monopolies form.
18th–19th centuries
Cultivation spreads and prices collapse. Spices become everyday goods.
06

Fine Art

Why it is valuable

Unique works, cultural prestige, and competition among the wealthy. Provenance and reputation drive prices into the millions.

Why it matters to someone just trying to get by

The art market is one of the clearest mirrors of extreme wealth concentration. When a painting sells for tens of millions while many people cannot raise transport fare, it is not abstract — it shows how money and status circulate in very different circles. For most people the practical takeaway is simpler: status purchases rarely solve cash-flow problems. Skills and income usually do.

How the value developed

Renaissance
Value tied to materials and the patron’s status.
19th–20th centuries
Artists become brands; modern market forms.
1980s–now
Contemporary art becomes a global asset class for the ultra-wealthy.
07

Fresh Water

Why it is valuable

Essential for life, farming and industry. Clean, reliable freshwater is already scarce in many places and becoming scarcer with climate stress and population growth.

Why it matters to someone just trying to get by

When water is scarce or expensive, food prices rise, small farms suffer, and household budgets take another hit. In many cities the cost of pure water or tanker supply is already a regular expense. Water is one of the most basic inputs that quietly decides how much of your money disappears before you even think about “luxuries.”

How the value developed

Ancient times
Civilisations form around reliable rivers and springs.
Industrial era
Large-scale water systems treat water as nearly free in many places.
21st century
Scarcity, pollution and climate stress raise both price and political tension.
08

Knowledge & Skills

Why it is valuable

Knowledge compounds. It raises productivity, creates new options, and cannot be taken away as easily as physical goods. High-quality, scarce skills still command the best pay.

Why it matters to someone just trying to get by

This is the one item on the list that most people can still improve without waiting for the system to change. A practical skill — phone repair, coding, sales, welding, digital marketing, accounting, driving, cooking at scale — is often the fastest realistic path from “I need money this week” to “I have more options next month.” Formal degrees help, but so do free or low-cost learning that actually produces income. Knowledge is the only asset here that you can grow while you are still short of cash.

How the value developed

Ancient world
Knowledge concentrated in libraries and elite schools.
15th century
Printing press makes knowledge far cheaper to copy.
19th–20th centuries
Mass education becomes a major driver of national wealth.
Today
Internet makes information free; specialised skills remain scarce and highly paid.
09

Land & Housing

Why it is valuable

Land is finite. Location, legal title and improvements create value. In growing cities it has been one of the most reliable long-term stores of wealth.

Why it matters to someone just trying to get by

Rent is often the single largest monthly expense. When land and housing prices rise faster than wages, more of every salary disappears into shelter. Understanding this helps explain why “just work harder” sometimes still leaves people stuck: the cost of the ground under their feet is rising. Secure tenure, even a small plot or a long-term rental strategy, is one of the practical ways people try to reduce that pressure.

How the value developed

Agricultural revolution
Control of fertile land becomes the base of wealth and power.
Industrial & urban eras
City land becomes extremely valuable; homeownership becomes a major wealth vehicle.
Today
Location still dominates. Housing costs remain a primary stress for most urban households.
10

Semiconductors (Chips)

Why they are valuable

The physical foundation of phones, banking apps, the internet, AI and almost every modern electronic device. Extremely complex and expensive to manufacture at the leading edge.

Why it matters to someone just trying to get by

Your phone, the banking app you use for transfers, the POS machine in the market, and the systems that decide credit scores all run on chips. When chip supply is tight, device prices rise and some services slow down. More importantly, the companies and countries that design and make advanced chips sit at the centre of today’s economy. Digital skills that use these tools (mobile money, online sales, content, basic coding) are among the practical ways ordinary people plug into that value without owning a factory.

How the value developed

1947–1960s
Transistor and integrated circuit invented. Electronics begin to shrink.
1970s–2000s
Personal computers, phones and the internet create mass demand.
2020s
AI boom and geopolitics turn advanced chips into strategic national assets.

Value is not only for the wealthy. These systems quietly set the prices, options and pressures that reach everyday life.
